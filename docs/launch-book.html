Audience

People in their first six weeks somewhere. Not "students".

"Students" is a group we can't reach. "Arrived in Bilbao in September" is a date, a place and an inbox. We target arrival, not age.

Core · now

Incoming exchange

Erasmus+ and US study abroad in Spain, 19–24, one semester or a year. They arrive on a known date, in waves, through an institution.

Next · spring

First-years who moved

Spanish students at university in a new city, 18–19. They arrive once, in September, and are harder to reach before they come.

Later

New in town

Language assistants, interns, first jobs. Same problem, no institution to reach them through.

Buyers, not users

Who pays

International offices, study abroad providers, ESN sections, student residences.

Why incoming exchange students first

  • The date is known. Every exchange student has an arrival week, often months in advance. That means we can reach them before they land.
  • They cluster. They come in two waves (September and January–February), live in the same few cities and go to the same few universities. A corner needs eleven people arriving in the same fortnight, and only this group guarantees that.
  • Institutions already email them. International offices and providers have an inbox for every arrival. One partner is a whole cohort.
  • We are them. Twelve of us arrived this month. We are the pilot cohort and the first content.

Who it isn't for

Tourists, people looking to date, and locals looking for a language exchange. The last one is a real demand we're parking, not rejecting. See vote 1.

How many

How big the first six weeks are.

Sourced figures only. Every number has its source next to it, so anyone arguing against this book can check it.

Spain, the market

59,271Erasmus+ higher-education students who came to Spain in 2024. The most of any country: Italy was next with 43,065.Erasmus+ Annual Report 2024, statistical annex, p.29–30 · SEPIE
36,826US students who studied abroad for credit in Spain in 2023/24. That's 12.4% of all US students abroad, the second-most popular destination after Italy, and Spain's highest ever.IIE Open Doors 2025 · opendoorsdata.org
159,002International students physically at Spanish universities in 2023-24: 63,624 on exchange and 95,378 doing a full degree.Ministerio de Ciencia, Innovación y Universidades, SIIU · ciencia.gob.es
403,878Erasmus+ higher-education student mobilities across all countries that started in 2024. This is the ceiling if Corners leaves Spain.Erasmus+ Annual Report 2024, statistical annex · op.europa.eu

Bilbao, the pilot

669Exchange students who arrived at UPV/EHU's Bizkaia campus in the first semester of 2024-25, out of 1,054 across the university. That's about 60 corners of eleven from one campus in one wave.EHU Campusa, 10 Sep 2024 · ehu.eus
600+International students welcomed by the University of Deusto in autumn 2025. The split between its Bilbao and San Sebastián campuses isn't verified.Deia, 4 Sep 2025 · deia.eus

Arrival waves at UPV/EHU: autumn classes started 8 September 2025, and spring classes 26 January 2026 (Faculty of Science and Technology calendar; other faculties vary by about a week). So the two windows to market in are late August to September and mid-to-late January. ESN Bilbao is the only ESN section listed for the Basque Country, and it's based at UPV/EHU.

The problem, measured

54.67%Exchange students who took part in no local activity during their stay: no club, association or volunteering. 17.16% said they weren't integrated into the local community.ESNsurvey XIV, about 11,100 responses, 2021 data collected during COVID · EAIE
2 in 3International students in an Australian study who had experienced loneliness or isolation, especially in their first months.Sawir et al., 2008, n=200 · Journal of Studies in International Education
76%People aged 15–24 in the EU who use Instagram, against 55% for TikTok (2023). This is where the campaign runs.European Parliament Research Service briefing, Dec 2025 · europarl.europa.eu

Two cautions before anyone quotes these. First, the loneliness studies are old and small. We haven't found a large Spain-specific one, so the pilot should measure it ourselves. Second, Spain's own count of US exchange students (8,698) is far below Open Doors' figure. A likely reason is that US-run programmes aren't recorded in Spanish university data. That's unverified, but it would mean providers like USAC are a separate channel from universities.

Positioning

One sentence, so twelve people say the same thing.

For people in their first six weeks in a new city, Corners is the app that puts you with eleven people who arrived when you did and one place to meet them each week. Unlike the group chat, you end up with names, not a member count.

Calendar

Launch on the course's dates. Then launch for real in January.

The four course blocks are fixed. The plan fits their dates instead of fighting them, and each graded deliverable is a real step for the company.

Risks

What breaks it, written down before it does.

RiskWhat we do about it
Strangers meeting strangers

Verified student emails, public places only, 18+, and one person reads every report within a day. This is written up before anyone meets.

Someone is already in Bilbao

Unera targets the same students in the same city. Our difference has to be visible in one sentence: a fixed group of eleven, dated to your arrival, meeting in person. If we can't say that in one sentence, we don't have a difference.

Not enough people

A corner needs eleven arrivals in the same fortnight. Launch one city, grow through institutions, and merge fortnights if a city is short.

Half the group doesn't show

Expect it. Eleven is chosen so that five or six still make a table. Measure turnout from week one.

Dead months

Arrivals come in two waves. Between them, we sell to institutions and build.

Personal data

Student emails are personal data under GDPR. Store the minimum, in the EU, and keep anything with a name out of the repo, as pitch 7 already says.

Speaking for an institution

Corners speaks in its own name. We never use USAC's or a university's name without written permission.

To vote · Tuesday

Six questions this book doesn't answer for you.

  1. Does a corner include one local?

    "Zero locals" is the thesis. But meeting locals is also the thing exchange students most want and least get. An eleventh seat for a local would change who the product is for.

    RecommendationPark until the pilot. Run one of the three pilot corners with a local and compare.

  2. Eleven per corner, or six?

    Eleven carries the brand's meaning. Six is easier to book a table for and harder to hide in.

    RecommendationEleven invited, expecting five or six at the table. Change it if turnout is above 80%.

  3. Students only, or anyone new in town?

    A student email is the cheapest safety check there is. Opening to everyone multiplies the market and the risk.

    RecommendationStudents only until January.

  4. Who pays?

    Students won't pay for their first six weeks. Institutions already pay for orientation.

    RecommendationFree for students, institutions pay per cohort. Build the model in block 4.

  5. What is the handle and the domain?

    "Corners" alone is almost certainly taken on Instagram and as a .com.

    RecommendationOperations checks availability before Tuesday and brings three options.

  6. App, or WhatsApp and a spreadsheet, for the pilot?

    Building first feels like progress. Running it by hand first tells us what to build.

    RecommendationBy hand until 22 October. It's zero build work, and it's what mini-project one is graded on.
