# The System Prompt

AI for Business · USAC Bilbao · Fall 2026

---

This is the only thing I put into your system.

You are going to build the company, and you are going to build the system that
runs it — including how each of you is evaluated. I am not writing that. This
document is my entire input, and it does two things: it tells you what you have
to end up knowing, and it tells you what USAC does not let us change.

Everything not in this document is yours to decide.

### How "knowing" is measured

One public table. Rows are people, columns are the twenty-one competencies.
Four levels:

| Level | Means | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | I have not touched it | — |
| **1** | I have seen it done | — |
| **2** | I have done it myself | There is a deliverable with my name on it |
| **3** | I taught it to someone, and they did it | There is that other person's deliverable |

**Level 2 is what USAC requires.** The syllabus says every student *will be able
to* do each of these. Level 2 is that sentence, with evidence.

**Level 3 is what the company requires of itself.** Because you do not know
something when you have done it. You know it when someone else can do it because
of you. That is the only standard that makes it impossible for the knowledge to
end up sitting in three people.

**Level 2 is what the top grade is made of.** USAC says every student *will be
able to* do each of these things; level 2 is that sentence with a deliverable
behind it. So the company's first objective — every member earns the top grade —
means, concretely: **everyone at level 2 in all twenty-one, with evidence.** It
needs an owner and a number, and you will give it both.

**Level 3 is not a second objective. It is the fastest way to hit the first
one.** The only way somebody moves from 1 to 2 is that somebody who is already
at 2 shows them how — and doing that is what puts the teacher at 3. A company
that reaches level 3 widely gets everyone to level 2 almost as a side effect.
Whether you push past that is your call: it is one of the objectives you set
for yourselves.

## Part 3 — Nine rules that do not change

### Four about how you are assessed

1. **Nobody can lower anybody's grade.** You can raise the standard as high as
   you want. You cannot turn this into a punishment system.
2. **Review before a date is unlimited; the date itself is not.** Bring me the
   work as many times as you want while you are building it — I am your client.
   When the delivery date arrives, the work is delivered.
3. **No anonymous work.** One person, one role, one deliverable a week, with
   your name on it.
4. **The company's first objective is that every one of its members earns the
   top grade** — which is the same as saying that everyone ends up knowing all
   of it. Not a wish: it gets an owner and it gets measured. Every other
   objective the company sets for itself.

### Five about how you organise

I am not giving you an org chart. I am giving you five rules, and the structure
is yours to invent inside them. They work the same whether you are seven people
or fourteen.

5. **Every obligation has a name behind it.** Five things have to exist: the
   Manual, a written record of decisions, the mastery table, and the final
   presentation. At any moment there is one specific person answering for each.
   What you call them, how you group them, whether one person covers two — yours.
6. **Nobody without a role.** Everyone answers for something.
7. **Roles rotate.** Nobody holds the same responsibility all term. How often and
   how the next person is chosen is yours.
8. **Every handover is written.** When a responsibility changes hands, there is a
   document. What goes in it is yours.
9. **The company answers for its own people.** If someone drops off, bringing
   them back is the group's job, not mine. How you do that goes in the Manual.

On that last one, so there is no confusion: attendance is still recorded, because
USAC requires it and that is not mine to waive. And if someone stops coming for
long enough, it stops being an internal matter and goes through USAC the normal
way. Everything before that point is yours.

## What is yours

Everything else. To be explicit, because people do not believe this the first
time they read it:

The whole structure. Who does what. What "done" means for every single deliverable.
What counts as participating. Who gives feedback and how. What happens when
somebody does not deliver, and how they get back on track. The pace. The tools
beyond the ones the syllabus names. What company you build. What you build for
it. How you teach each other. How you amend your own rules.

You write it. You ratify it. You enforce it. I check one thing: that it fits
inside Parts 1, 2 and 3.

If it fits, it stands — including the parts I would have done differently.
