# The matrix is not a document any more

It used to be a table in a file that somebody updated at the end of every
session, and then read to spot the gaps. That last step is the part that never
happens: a 21 × 11 grid has 231 cells, and nobody spots a pattern in 231 cells at
16:25 on a Thursday.

**It is the Teams screen of the board.** Open the board, click any square, claim a
level. The gaps are then calculated rather than noticed:

- competences where **nobody** is above level 1 — what this company cannot do yet
- competences held by **exactly one person** — the single points of failure, and
  the reason to care is November, not politeness
- people with **no level 3 anywhere** — nobody they have taught

## The levels

| | |
|---|---|
| **0** | not touched |
| **1** | I have seen it done |
| **2** | **I have done it** — a deliverable carries my name |
| **3** | **I taught it and they did it** — their deliverable is the proof |

**Level 2 is what USAC requires. Level 3 is what this company requires of
itself**, and that is not ambition for its own sake: the inherited objective is
that *all eleven* demonstrate all 21 competences, and that is only reachable if
the people who can do a thing teach it. A company where three people are good at
everything fails that objective by arithmetic.

**Above level 1, a claim needs proof** — a link or a filename. Not because anybody
is suspected of lying, but because a claim with nothing behind it is worth
nothing to the person reading the grid to decide who should do the next thing.

## What still belongs in this folder

The proof, when it is not a link: a file, an export, a screenshot of something
that will not exist in December. Name it after the competence and the person.
