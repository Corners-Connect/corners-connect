# The four required deliverables — what is actually being asked

These four come from the USAC syllabus and their content is not ours to change.
What "good" looks like inside each one is yours to define in the Manual; **what
has to be in the box is fixed**, and it is copied here so nobody has to guess.

---

## 1 · Prompt lab and brief case critique
**Due: Tuesday 22 September (session 5)** · Case Studies

- [ ] A prompt lab: the prompts you built, what you were trying to get, what you
      changed and why
- [ ] A short critique of an ethics case, applied to your own company
- [ ] Every tool named, generated content marked, sources cited
- [ ] Your name on it

*This is the first thing you hand in, and it sets the standard for everything
after. Whatever the Manual says "done" means, this is where you find out whether
you wrote that definition well enough.*

---

## 2 · Mini-Project 1 — the launch
**Due: Thursday 22 October (session 13)** · 20% of the grade

- [ ] At least one image, made and directed by you
- [ ] At least one short video
- [ ] An SEO brief
- [ ] SEM variants
- [ ] **The reasoning that ties every choice to a piece of data or a constraint**
- [ ] Who made what, on every piece
- [ ] Tools named and generated content marked

*The fifth line is the one people underestimate. Beautiful assets with no argument
behind them are a portfolio, not a launch. The syllabus asks for the argument.*

---

## 3 · Mini-Project 2 — the machine
**Due: Tuesday 10 November (session 18)** · 20% of the grade

- [ ] A vibe-coded micro-app that runs
- [ ] Connected to a model and to data
- [ ] A Make or n8n scenario: capture → enrich with AI → store → notify
- [ ] A one-page readme containing:
  - [ ] a diagram of the flow
  - [ ] the key prompts
  - [ ] cost notes — what it costs to run, honestly
  - [ ] log notes — what happens when it fails
- [ ] Who wrote what
- [ ] **Invented or your own data only.** Nothing belonging to real third parties

*The readme is one page. That is a constraint, not a suggestion: if the flow cannot
be explained on one page, it is more complicated than the problem deserves.*

---

## 4 · Final project — the company
**Due: Thursday 3 December (session 25)** · 20% of the grade

- [ ] The business model: what this is, who pays or would pay, how it scales, what
      the risks are
- [ ] Go-to-market
- [ ] KPIs and a test plan
- [ ] An ROI model with real costs — tokens, tools, time
- [ ] **What you learned building it, including what did not work**
- [ ] Presented in class, sessions 24 and 25
- [ ] Each of you answers for your own area under questioning

*The fifth line is not a closing courtesy. A company that never took off and
explains clearly why is worth exactly as much here as one that made money, and
that is not a consolation — it is how the course is graded.*

---

## Two things that apply to all four

**The date is real; the review before it is unlimited.** While you are building,
show the work to the client as often as you want and he will tell you whether it
meets the standard. When the date arrives, it ships.

**Nothing is anonymous.** Every deliverable says who made what and which tool did
what. Not because of the rules, though the rules do say so — because being unable
to point at your own contribution is a real problem, not an academic one.
